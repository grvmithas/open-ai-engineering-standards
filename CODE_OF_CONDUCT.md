# Code of Conduct

**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Last updated:** 2026-07-16

## Purpose

This policy defines the behavior required to participate in AIES. It protects technical disagreement while prohibiting conduct that makes collaboration unsafe or exclusionary.

## Why

An engineering standard is credible only when contributors can challenge claims, evidence, and designs without being attacked personally. Maintainers enforce this boundary consistently across repository and community spaces.

## How

### Required conduct

- Critique artifacts and decisions, not people.
- Support factual claims with reproducible evidence or primary sources.
- Respect names, pronouns, privacy, and professional boundaries.
- Accept maintainer direction on scope, moderation, and repository safety.
- Disclose conflicts of interest that could bias a proposal or review.

### Prohibited conduct

Harassment, threats, discrimination, sexualized attention, doxxing, sustained disruption, impersonation, and publishing private communications without permission are prohibited.

### Reporting and enforcement

Report incidents privately using the security contact in `SECURITY.md` when disclosure could expose a person. Include links, timestamps, and relevant context. Maintainers will acknowledge a report within five business days, limit information to reviewers who need it, and prohibit retaliation.

| Severity | Maintainer response |
|---|---|
| Correctable disruption | Private warning and required correction |
| Repeated or material misconduct | Temporary participation restriction |
| Threat, harassment, doxxing, or evasion | Permanent ban and platform escalation |

An accused maintainer must recuse from investigation and appeal. Appeals go to an uninvolved maintainer and must identify a procedural or factual error.

## Tradeoffs

- Private handling protects reporters but limits public transparency.
- Firm enforcement can remove technically valuable contributors; community safety takes precedence.
- Case summaries improve accountability but must not expose affected people.

## Anti-patterns

- **Public incident trials:** they amplify harm and compromise evidence.
- **Status-based exceptions:** influence does not reduce enforcement.
- **Forced reconciliation:** reporters are never required to contact the reported person.

## Enterprise considerations

Organization-sponsored contributors remain subject to employer policies and this policy. Maintainers preserve only the minimum incident records needed for enforcement, legal obligations, and repeat-offender detection.

## Checklist

- [ ] Reports have a private channel.
- [ ] Conflicted maintainers recuse.
- [ ] Actions are proportional and documented.
- [ ] Reporter confidentiality is preserved.
- [ ] Retaliation is treated as a separate violation.
